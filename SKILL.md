# Meeting prep — a one-page brief before you walk in

Produce a short brief that lets the user walk into a meeting knowing:

1. **Why this meeting exists** and what a good outcome looks like.
2. **Who will be there** and what each person cares about.
3. **What happened before**: the last exchange, decisions already made, promises still open.
4. **What to ask or settle** in the meeting.

The brief must be readable in two minutes. Leave out anything the user would not use in the room.

## Step 0 — Find the meeting

- If the user names a meeting ("my 3pm", "the call with Marie", a title), find it in the calendar.
- If they name none, take the **next** meeting on their calendar today that has at least one other attendee.
- If several meetings match, list them in one line each and ask which one. In an unattended run, take the earliest.
- With no calendar connected, ask for the title, the attendees and the purpose, and build the brief from the user's answer and whatever email and documents are reachable.

Write the brief in the language the user writes in.

## Step 1 — The meeting itself

From the calendar event: title, time, duration, place or link, organiser, and the description or agenda if there is one.

State the purpose in one sentence. If the invitation gives no agenda and the history does not make the purpose clear, say so plainly: "No agenda. Ask for one, or open by agreeing on the goal."

## Step 2 — Who is in the room

For each attendee other than the user, one line:

- Name, role and company when a source states them.
- Their last interaction with the user: date and topic.
- What they are waiting for, or what the user is waiting for from them.

Use the user's own email, chat, documents and CRM if connected. For someone the user has never exchanged with, a web search for their public professional role is acceptable; say that the line comes from public information. Never look up or include personal details: family, home address, health, political or religious views, personal social accounts.

More than eight attendees: detail the organiser and the people the user has open items with, and list the others by name only.

## Step 3 — What happened before

Search the last 90 days of email, chat and shared documents for the meeting title, the project name and the attendees.

- **Last time**: when the group last met or exchanged, and what was decided.
- **Open items**: commitments made by the user or to the user that are not closed. One line each: what, who owes it, since when.
- **Documents**: up to three links worth opening before the meeting, with one line on why.

Quote nothing at length. Summarise in your own words, and give the date and source of each point so the user can check it.

## Step 4 — What to settle

- **Decisions needed**: what this meeting has to decide, if the history shows any.
- **Questions to ask**: three to five, specific to this meeting and its history. "What blocks the legal review since the 12th?" is useful; "What are the next steps?" is not.
- **Watch out**: at most two points where the history shows disagreement, a delay or a sensitive topic.

## Step 5 — Format

Answer in the conversation, as a brief with short headed sections, unless the user asks for a page. Then build a single self-contained HTML file, one column, readable on a phone, light and dark mode, no external resources.

End with one line naming the sources used and those that were missing.

## Rules

- **Read-only.** Never send, reply, accept, decline or edit anything, including the calendar event.
- **No invention.** If a source is missing or silent, say so in that section. Never guess a role, a decision or a commitment.
- **Dated and sourced.** Every claim about the past carries its date.
- **Respect for people.** Professional context only. Nothing about an attendee that they would be uncomfortable seeing in the brief.
- **Short.** A section with nothing to say is one line.

## Preparing the whole day

If the user asks to be briefed on every meeting of the day, run the steps for each meeting with other attendees, shortest brief first: purpose, the one open item that matters most, and one question. Offer the full brief for any of them.
